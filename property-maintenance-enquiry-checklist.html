Six steps to triage commercial maintenance enquiries

Reactive repairs and planned facility maintenance need different response paths. A damaged water main at an occupied commercial property should not wait behind a general painting quote. This checklist helps operators manage incoming requests without making promises the team cannot keep.

1. Identify urgent health, safety or operational risks

Flag flooding, exposed wiring, structural hazards, blocked emergency access and loss of essential services for immediate human review. The correct escalation depends on your team, contract and safety responsibilities; do not allow software alone to decide site safety.

2. Record the requesting party and location

  • Customer or property-management business and authorized contact.
  • Site name, suburb, building access instructions and operating hours.
  • Preferred reply channel and any contact restrictions.

3. Collect enough detail for dispatch

Ask for the trade or type of issue, description, time first noticed, scope of impact and optional photos. Do not request identity documents or unrelated tenant information. Restrict access to uploaded images and delete them when no longer needed.

4. Establish approval and service entitlement

Distinguish an existing SLA client from an ad hoc request. Confirm whether the person is authorized to approve work, whether a site visit or quote must precede repairs, and what pricing approvals are required.

5. Assign an accountable next step

Use new → triaged → awaiting approval → scheduled → in progress → completed. Every request needs an owner, an update interval and a completion note. If your scheduling or messaging connection fails, have an explicit manual fallback.

Example acknowledgement (human-reviewed)

“Thank you for reporting the issue. Please confirm the property address, whether the area is safe, the best site contact and whether you need an inspection or an urgent repair. Our team will review availability and confirm the next step.”

This is sample copy. It is not an automated emergency response system.

6. Review service outcomes

Measure time to triage, percentage with an owner, repeat visits, unresolved approval delays, and service complaints. Do not claim a performance improvement until it has been measured against a comparable baseline.
