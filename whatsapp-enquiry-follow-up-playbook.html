WhatsApp enquiries: a practical follow-up playbook

When a customer messages “Can you give me a price?”, the business needs a reliable handoff—not an automated sales pitch. This guide explains how a small team can respond to service enquiries, collect only useful details, track quotes and follow up without confusing a customer's request with permission for future marketing.

Scope and limitations

This is a manual operating procedure that can be used with your existing approved business communications channels. LeadLift does not claim that WhatsApp is integrated, that messages are sent automatically, or that a response-time or conversion improvement has been measured. The templates below require staff review.

1. Decide who owns the inbox during business hours

Assign a primary person and a backup for each staffed period. Publish the hours customers can reasonably expect a response. Route urgent safety or service-disruption messages to a human promptly; never let a lead score decide whether an emergency needs attention. When the owner is absent, transfer responsibility explicitly rather than leaving an unread conversation in a shared phone.

Minimum handoff record

  • When the enquiry arrived and its source.
  • Who is responsible for the next response.
  • What service or product the person requested.
  • What essential information is still missing.
  • When the next promised update is due.

Do not capture by default

  • ID numbers, card details, passwords or unrelated documents.
  • Customer contact lists copied into a marketing tool.
  • Medical, financial or other sensitive facts not needed for the service.
  • Attachments retained without a business purpose or retention rule.

3. Use short, reviewed replies that create a next step

Template A — new quote request

“Thanks for contacting [Business]. We can review your request for [service]. Could you confirm [two essential details] and your preferred location or timeframe? We'll check the scope and let you know the next step. This is not yet a confirmed quote or booking.”

Template B — update when a quote needs checking

“We're still checking [parts availability / site requirements / scheduling]. We expect to update you by [realistic date and time]. If that changes, we'll let you know. Thank you for your patience.”

Template C — service-specific follow-up

“Following up on the [specific service] quote you requested on [date]. Would you like us to clarify anything or close the enquiry for now? We won't assume you want further reminders.”

Use these only for the enquiry the customer initiated and where the communication is appropriate and lawful. Stop when the person refuses further contact. Do not copy a template into an unsolicited promotional campaign.

4. Separate service communication from direct marketing

Someone asking for a quote may need a response about that quote; this is not blanket consent to promotional broadcasts. Maintain a separate, evidence-backed permission record for electronic direct marketing, honour refusals, and do not import old customer or publicly visible numbers into marketing lists. POPIA section 69 imposes specific requirements on unsolicited electronic direct marketing. Obtain legal review of any proposed consent-request campaign, including the prescribed form and existing-customer exception, before sending it.

WhatsApp is not automatically a compliant outreach channel.

Use an authorized business account and the applicable platform rules. Do not send bulk cold messages, repeat one-time consent requests or use personal profiles to promote LeadLift. This guide does not provide a marketing permission or platform integration.

5. Keep a small, auditable enquiry pipeline

Use new → needs details → quote in progress → quote sent → awaiting decision → booked / closed as a manual operating model. Record the actual date a quote was sent and who approved it. A quote is not a sale; a checkout redirect is not a payment; a tentative booking is not a completed job. Never count a message, proposal, sale or payment without corresponding evidence.

6. Define a follow-up rule before sending anything

For each active requested-service enquiry, write down the next action, owner and due date. If a customer gives a preferred time or channel, use that. A possible internal starting rule is to review open quotes once on the next staffed day and close or pause unresolved records after a clearly documented interval. This is a suggested workflow—not a legal safe harbour or permission to keep contacting someone who has refused.

7. Measure outcomes from records, not assumptions

Weekly service measures

  • First meaningful response time: time from arrival to the first useful human response, measured within stated staffed hours.
  • Assigned-owner rate: enquiries with a named owner divided by genuine enquiries.
  • Overdue next actions: open enquiries whose promised update time has passed.

Weekly commercial measures

  • Quote-to-booking rate: confirmed bookings divided by quotes sent in a consistent cohort.
  • Win rate: confirmed wins divided by decided opportunities, with a documented definition.
  • Cash received: only settled or independently verified receipts, not payment-link visits.

Keep sample and test records separate from customer metrics. Compare like-for-like periods before concluding that any change improved sales. A short weekly review is more useful than a dashboard filled with guessed outcomes.

8. Run a safe handoff test

  1. Send a clearly labelled internal test enquiry through your authorized channel.
  2. Check who receives it and whether the owner and timestamp are captured.
  3. Ask the backup staff member to locate the record without access to unrelated private customer data.
  4. Verify that an urgent message reaches a human, and that a missing-information request does not invent a quote.
  5. Test a refusal: the record must stop any further marketing attempts.
  6. Remove or retain the test record according to the team's documented policy.
