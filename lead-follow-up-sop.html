A lead follow-up SOP your team can actually use

A repeatable enquiry process does not require complicated software. Start with a named owner, a realistic response target, clear escalation and a weekly review. Use this template for enquiries that people have genuinely submitted to your business—not for unsolicited marketing.

Use this as a starting point, not a promise. The response windows below are illustrative internal targets. Adapt them to opening hours, staffing, urgency and the expectations you set with customers. This page does not collect or transmit customer information.

1. Decide who owns each enquiry

Assign one primary owner and one backup for every intake channel: website form, telephone, email and any messaging channel your business has actually authorized. The owner is responsible for the next action even when software creates the record.

2. Use a simple triage rule

Enquiry typeExample internal targetNext action
Safety-critical or service outageImmediate human review during staffed hoursRoute to the authorized emergency or technical team. Do not let an automated quote imply a safety assessment.
Ready for a site visit or quotationWithin 2 staffed hoursConfirm location, scope, preferred contact method and a realistic appointment window.
General pricing or exploratory questionBy the next staffed business dayAnswer the question, request only necessary details and agree a follow-up if requested.
Incomplete or duplicate submissionDuring the next triage reviewCheck for an existing conversation before sending a duplicate response.

These are sample operating targets, not industry benchmarks or guaranteed response times. Publish only targets your team can meet.

3. Record only what is needed

For a new enquiry, record the request reference, channel, received time, business-relevant service details, contact details supplied by the person, preferred reply channel, assigned owner, next action and status. Restrict access to staff who need it. Avoid collecting sensitive or unnecessary information just because a form permits it.

Minimal pipeline

New → Assigned → Waiting for customer / Next action booked → Quote or service step → Closed

Every open record needs an owner and a next-action date. A record marked “waiting” should still have a review date; otherwise enquiries quietly disappear.

Example acknowledgement

Thanks for contacting us about [requested service]. We have received your enquiry and [team member or team] will review it during [stated business hours]. If you can share [one essential missing detail], that will help us direct your request. Please do not send sensitive information through this form.

Use this only to respond to the enquiry the person initiated. A service reply is not permission to subscribe them to unrelated promotional campaigns.

4. Escalate before the promise is missed

  1. Check unassigned records at the start and midpoint of each staffed shift.
  2. Flag records approaching their stated response target and route them to the backup owner.
  3. Escalate safety-critical or time-sensitive requests to a human with the necessary authority.
  4. Do not automatically send repeated follow-ups after an objection or a request to stop marketing. Record the objection and respect it across channels.
  5. Close the loop with a recorded outcome; never mark a deal won merely because a quote was sent.

5. Review a weekly scorecard

Use a fixed reporting period and a consistent definition for each metric. Exclude test records, duplicates and spam from business conversion reporting, but retain a separate technical count for diagnosing intake failures.

First meaningful response timeMedian of first human-useful reply time minus received timeHow long people wait; specify staffed vs elapsed hours

Keep the underlying definitions unchanged when comparing weeks. Seasonal demand, case mix and staffing changes can affect results; do not attribute improvement to a tool without evidence.

6. Run a 30-minute pilot

  1. Minutes 0–10: Map where real enquiries arrive and assign an owner/backup for each channel.
  2. Minutes 10–20: Choose realistic staffed-hour targets and write one approved acknowledgement.
  3. Minutes 20–30: Walk through three fictional scenarios: urgent request, missing details and duplicate form. Confirm escalation, opt-out handling and record access.

Privacy and permission boundaries

Answering a person's specific service request is not blanket permission for electronic direct marketing. Before any separate marketing message, check the applicable POPIA Section 69 requirements, existing-customer exception where relevant, prior refusals, identity disclosure and a workable opt-out. Do not reuse the enquiry list as a cold-email list. Document retention, access and deletion procedures suitable for your business.
